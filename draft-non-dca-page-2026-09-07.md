## Page title

Non-Discretionary Car Finance Commission Claims: Were You Fully Informed?

## Meta description / excerpt

Paid high hidden commission on your car finance? Non-discretionary commission claims explained, including the FCA's 39% test. Check your claim with Allegiant.

## Body copy

Not all car finance commission was the banned discretionary kind. Most agreements paid the dealer or broker a fixed commission instead. These are called non-discretionary commission arrangements. If that commission was high and nobody clearly told you about it, you may be owed money under the FCA's motor finance redress scheme.

### What Is a Non-Discretionary Commission Arrangement?

With a non-discretionary arrangement, the lender decided what commission the dealer or broker earned. It was usually a flat amount or a fixed percentage of what you borrowed. The dealer could not raise your interest rate to earn more. That was a discretionary commission arrangement (DCA), and the FCA banned those in January 2021.

Non-discretionary commission was, and still is, allowed. The problem is what you were told. You should have been clearly informed that commission was being paid and given the information that mattered about it. In August 2025 the Supreme Court decided that a large commission, hidden from the customer, can make the relationship between you and the lender unfair. Where it does, you are entitled to money back.

### When Does Non-Discretionary Commission Give You a Claim?

The FCA confirmed the motor finance redress scheme on 30 March 2026 (policy statement PS26/3). Under the scheme, your agreement is treated as unfair where the commission was high and was not properly disclosed to you. The FCA counts commission as high where it was both:

- 39% or more of the total cost of credit (the interest and charges you paid), and
- 10% or more of the amount you borrowed

Here is an example. You borrowed £10,000 and the interest and charges came to £4,000. If the dealer's commission was £1,560 or more, and nobody clearly told you about it, your agreement is presumed unfair under the scheme.

An agreement can also qualify where the lender had undisclosed ties with the dealer, such as a deal giving that lender first refusal on your finance.

Very small commissions are excluded. No redress is payable where the commission was under £120 on agreements before April 2014, or under £150 from April 2014 onwards.

### Which Agreements Are Covered?

The scheme covers car finance agreements such as personal contract purchase (PCP) and hire purchase (HP) taken out between 6 April 2007 and 1 November 2024, on new or used vehicles. It includes agreements you have already paid off. The FCA expects around 12.1 million agreements to be in scope across both commission types.

### Where the Scheme Stands Right Now

Parts of the scheme have been paused since 2 July 2026 while the Upper Tribunal hears a legal challenge brought by several lenders and a consumer group. Until the tribunal decides, lenders do not have to calculate or pay compensation. The hearing is due in December 2026 or early 2027.

Lenders must still log complaints and gather the commission data the scheme needs. You can still complain now, and doing so means your claim is ready to move as soon as the pause lifts.

The FCA estimates average compensation of around £830 per eligible agreement. The highest commission cases may get the full commission back plus interest. Most claims are expected to be settled by the end of 2027, subject to the tribunal timetable. Check the FCA's website or our blog for the latest position.

### What Is the Difference Between a DCA Claim and a Non-Discretionary Claim?

A DCA claim is about a dealer who could push your interest rate up to earn more commission. A non-discretionary claim is about a fixed commission that was high and hidden. Many people do not know which type their agreement used, and if you had more than one agreement you may have both. We check which applies as part of your claim.
